\subsection{Which Views the Fat-Handedness Objection Reaches}

The fat-handedness objection reaches two views in the atlas and passes two
others by.  \citet{baumgartner2016} argue that Craver's mutual-manipulability
criterion, embedded in Woodward's interventionism, fails for every genuine
whole--part pair.  An ideal intervention must reach the phenomenon through
exactly one route.  A phenomenon's constituents are its supervenience base, so
any change to the phenomenon carries a change in some constituent occupying the
same spatiotemporal region.  The intervention therefore reaches both.  Woodward
calls this fat-handedness: ideality requires a route to one variable, and a
common cause has two.  An impossibility proof follows, and a dilemma with it.
Either constitutive relevance lies beyond empirical reach, or constitution
collapses into causation.

The objection needs a whole--part relation, and only some views supply one.
Under the object view a circuit is a named component set, warranted by ablating
members and reading model behavior.  Under the role view the warrant comes from
interchange interventions on those same components.  Each fixes identity by
manipulating the constituents of the behavior those constituents realize, which
is the configuration the objection targets.  The structural view individuates by
gauge-orbit membership and takes the weight matrix as its evidence, read without
touching any behavior.  The instrumental view claims predictive equivalence and
stops there.  The objection passes these two by.

Consider a paper writing ``circuit $C$ implements indirect object
identification.''  It can mean that the components of $C$ carry causal
dependencies among themselves, a within-level claim that Woodwardian
intervention supports.  It can also mean that the joint activity of $C$ realizes
the behavior, a whole--part claim that inherits the objection.
\citet{wang2022} establish their circuit by patching, which warrants the first
reading, and then describe it as implementing the behavior, which invites the
second; \S\ref{sec:motivation} traces a related conflation in the same result.
Declaring the view separates the readings, and the declaration settles whether
Baumgartner and Gebharter's objection applies to the paper at hand.

\citet{caoyamins2024a} argue that runnability forces detail.  Making a model
execute the capacity ``can end up forcing the preservation of many internal
details''; more of them may matter ``than we would have guessed from the
armchair.''  Runnability does force detail, and it forces detail into the
model.  The nearest treatment inside interpretability constrains the same
thing: \citet{geiger2023} hold, after \citet{yablo1992}, that a high-level
variable's value range should be grained so that cited causes stay proportional
to their effects.  Both proposals discipline the description.  Resolving power
disciplines the measurement.  A model may carve its variables proportionally and
still be probed badly, by one method too coarse to separate the values the model
posits, or by another fine enough to report distinctions the network collapses.
Description detail and measurement discrimination therefore vary independently,
and over-resolution is a failure of the second.

Interpretability has already reported this underdetermination and stopped at
reporting it.  \citet{milliere2024} find that interchange interventions license
several representational readings of one network, and that those readings can be
mutually exclusive.  Treating the strongest of them as correct, they conclude,
``requires further justification.''  One intervention family, applied to one
network, licenses incompatible readings, and the report ends there.  The axes of
\S\ref{sec:axes} say what the readings disagree about: two interpretations
assigning identical components and divergent roles disagree on the identity
criterion, and criterion choice lies outside what interchange interventions have
been shown to settle.  Settling it takes a declaration, which is what a view
report supplies.
